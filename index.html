A short course

Healthy Skepticism

Making sense of medical claims. Four sessions on what the numbers in a study, a screening test or a drug advertisement actually say, so you can have a better conversation with your doctor.

What the course is

Medical claims arrive as numbers. A drug cuts your risk by 36%. A test is 99% accurate. A finding is statistically significant. Each of those sentences is true and none of them answers the question you actually have.

The course works through five ways numbers mislead, one at a time, with real studies and real advertisements. Everything is done by counting. There is no mathematics beyond arithmetic, and no assumption that you have seen any statistics before.

Taught by Murray Cantor, PhD, and Joel Keenan, MD.

The five faults

  1. Relative reporting What does a 36% improvement from taking a drug really mean?
  2. Probability reversal What does a negative COVID test really mean?
  3. Arbitrary categories How concerned should you be if your systolic blood pressure goes from 119 to 120?
  4. The Bernoulli fallacy Why does the published medical advice keep changing?
  5. The causal leap Does bird watching prevent dementia?
